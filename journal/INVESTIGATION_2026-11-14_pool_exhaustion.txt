PART A -- THE MECHANICAL TEST

-- A.1 --
theory_x/stage6_fountain/generator.py, _build_recent_striking_block(),
lines 2682-2688:
  now = time.time()
  tag_rows = self._conversations_reader.read(
      "SELECT fountain_event_id, score FROM genius_tags "
      "WHERE class = 'STRIKING' AND tagged_at > ? AND tagged_at < ? "
      "ORDER BY score DESC LIMIT 200",
      (now - 24 * 3600, now - 300),
  )
Exact limit: a 24-HOUR lookback window (86400 seconds), excluding the
most recent 300 seconds (5 minutes), filtered to genius_tags rows whose
class is exactly 'STRIKING'. Not a fire-count window, not a tag-expiry
field, not a magnitude -- a plain two-sided timestamp range on `tagged_at`.
LIMIT 200 is a cap on how many rows the query can return, not the window
itself (and, per A.4 below, the cap was never actually reached -- far
fewer than 200 rows existed).

-- A.2 --
Converting to fires at the incident night's real cadence (fires landing
roughly every 2 minutes around the incident, confirmed directly: fire
3261 at 22:29:19, fire 3265 at 22:37:29, fire 3271 at 22:49:51 -- about
2-2.1 min/fire throughout):
  24 hours = 1440 minutes =~ 720 fires at that cadence.
  Observed lag (topic onset at fire 3261 to first same-topic quote at
  fire 3271): 10 fires, ~20.5 minutes (22:29:19 to 22:49:51).
  Ratio: the real window is ~72x longer than the observed lag in both
  fire-count and minute terms (1440/20.5 =~ 70; 720/10 = 72).

-- A.3 -- verdict, stated plainly, not softened --
NO. The window size does not land anywhere close to 10 fires / 20

    AND tagged_at > (fire_3271_ts - 86400) AND tagged_at < (fire_3271_ts
    - 300) ORDER BY score DESC LIMIT 200
  Result: 10 rows (not 200 -- the real constraint is simply how few fires
  had been tagged STRIKING in that whole 24h span, not the LIMIT clause).
