=============================================================================
A BUG CAUGHT BEFORE SHIPPING
=============================================================================
The first version of _record_focus_rotation_shadow used an UPDATE keyed on
"WHERE id = (SELECT MAX(id) FROM topic_gate_shadow_log)" to attach its
columns onto the row _record_topic_gate_shadow had just inserted for the
same fire. Caught on review, before any live or test run: if
_record_topic_gate_shadow's own INSERT ever failed (it has its own
try/except and silently no-ops on failure, same discipline as this new
method), the UPDATE would silently corrupt a PREVIOUS fire's row instead
of doing nothing. Fixed by making _record_focus_rotation_shadow INSERT its
own independent row instead -- the two instruments now cannot corrupt each
other's data even if one of them hits its own exception branch. (The
table's one pre-existing NOT NULL column, would_fire, has no default, so
this instrument's rows set it to 0 with a comment explaining that 0 here
means "not a topic-gate-overlap row," not a real would-fire=False
observation -- flagged here so a future reader querying would_fire alone
doesn't misread these rows.)

WHAT THIS DATA STILL CAN'T TELL US
=============================================================================
- With exactly ONE investigated incident, there is no way to statistically
  calibrate a detection threshold against true positives/negatives -- K=6
  and the 70% backstop were chosen because they sit in the rare tail of
  ORDINARY variation, not because they are known to catch real problems.
  By construction, they would NOT have caught the one real incident this
  whole investigation started from (streak=4, rate=60%, both common).
  Picking a K that WOULD catch the real incident (e.g. K=4) would also
  fire on ~1 in 5 ordinary windows, including legitimate multi-fire
  engagement with a single real paper or story (the streak=10 mouse-
  neocortex window is the clearest real example found).
- This means the honest state of the detector today is: good at flagging
  genuinely rare, extreme dwell (K=6/70% catches the tail), but NOT
  demonstrated to catch incidents shaped like the one that started this
  investigation. Those may need a qualitatively different signal (e.g.
  cross-branch topic entanglement, which is what actually made 11-10's
  incident unusual per groove_facts.txt, not raw repetition count) rather
  than a stricter version of the same repetition count.
- How much more shadow-logging time before a live flip is justified: this
  round's replay shows the EXISTING rotation actuator's own trigger
  essentially never fires at the fountain's real ~2min cadence within a
  68-minute/40-fire window unless a rut is both extreme AND dense. Getting
  enough real triggered rows at K=6/70% to judge whether rotation's
  candidate-pool exclusion behaves sanely in production would, at the
  observed ~1.7-2.6% ordinary incidence, need on the order of several
  hundred fires of real flag-on logging (roughly 1-2 weeks at current
  cadence) before there would be enough triggered rows to look at, not
  just zero or one.
- The two real corroboration events found (1.4) are too few (2, neither
  inside a repetition incident) to validate that the corroboration-count
  column behaves correctly under real multi-channel repetition pressure,
  as opposed to just the isolated-fixture test above.
