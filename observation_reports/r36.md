### 0.1 Limits of a retrospective on this data — plainly

1. **No control periods exist.** Every window contains either an intervention, a reboot, or both. Nothing here is a controlled comparison; every "effect" is a before/after on a system that was also changing for other reasons.
2. **The feedback loops make attribution circular.** Fires feed beliefs feed prompts feed fires. A change to any of these shows up in all of them within a day, so "X moved after we changed Y" is weak evidence even when the timing is exact.
3. **n is small at the day level.** ~500 fires/day sounds like a lot; split by mode and gated to item-bearing fires it is 60–330/day, giving CIs ±5–8pt. Most day-to-day movement in this report is inside the noise.
4. **Twelve-plus changes in 12 days.** R23–R35 shipped faster than any endpoint's settling time. Several windows contain two changes.
5. **Multiple comparisons.** This report computes ~30 series and 7 correlations. At p<0.05 one or two apparent findings are expected by chance. I have marked which ones I would bet on and which I would not.
6. **The most important series is 6 days long.** See 0.3.
7. **Survivorship in the record.** `journal/` and `CARRY_OVER.md` record what was noticed. Things nobody looked at leave no trace, so "no round noted X" is not evidence that X was absent.

### C3 Did anything get WORSE that nobody tracked?

**Yes, and it is the finding of this round.**

**R29 inflated maxDF\*, and R31–R32 then spent two rounds chasing the inflation.**

Crystallized-belief length across R29 (OBSERVED): mean **112 → 313**, median **80 → 369**, max **299 → 600** — the ceiling change, exactly as designed. But maxDF\* counts *document frequency*, and longer documents contain more tokens, so any token's DF rises mechanically. Truncating both periods to a common length isolates it:

| documents truncated to | pre-R29 | post-R29 | step |
|---|---|---|---|
| full text (as shipped) | 12.6% | 21.4% | **+8.8 pt** |
| first 300 chars (the pre-R29 ceiling) | 12.6% | 18.8% | +6.3 pt |
| first 150 chars | 12.3% | 16.3% | **+4.0 pt** |

**Roughly 30–55% of the maxDF\* step at R29 is a length artefact, not convergence.** A real +4 to +6 pt step survives, so the alarm was not false — but it was amplified by our own change, and nothing in R30–R32 accounts for that.

**Worse: the maxDF\* register-exclusion list is fitted across the regime change it is meant to measure.** `register_exclusion.json` declares `window_utc 2026-06-20 → 2026-08-04`, n=1101 — a 45-day window of which only the **final ~1.4 days are post-R29**. The exclusion terms are therefore fitted on short pre-R29 documents and applied to long post-R29 ones. The instrument has a covariate shift baked into it.

**A second thing that got worse, and it is my error.** In R32 I wrote that the groove filter "has been an exact no-op for the whole contamination window." **That is wrong.** `groove_alerts` holds **81,786 rows, 81,592 at severity ≥ 0.5, 62 in the last 24 h, most recent 0.0 days ago.** The dynamic half of the filter was live and firing throughout. Recent patterns:

```
08-08 04:34 sev=1.00  'between readings / readings feels / the quiet'
08-08 03:51 sev=1.00  'lean towards / i lean / to particulars'
08-08 04:12 sev=0.90  'complicate seemingly / we complicate / often we'
```

What I verified was narrower: the four **static** `_CHRONIC_GROOVE` terms matched 0/240 replayed slots. The dynamic terms are volatile trigram fragments of the most recent fires; they never matched the Gatwick preamble, but they **were excluding other candidates**.

**SPECULATIVE and worth testing:** if the filter removed non-contaminant candidates from a 10-slot pool while never removing the contaminant, it was *actively concentrating* the pool on the contaminant. That would make the pre-R32 groove filter a **contributing cause** of the mono-culture rather than a failed defence against it. This is testable by replaying `groove_alerts` against historical pools; I did not do it this round.

### C5 The most likely thing still wrong that we have not looked at

**SPECULATIVE, and it is where I would look next: the genius scorer is drifting against a corpus its training set no longer describes, and it is silently steering everything downstream.**

The evidence, assembled from pieces that no single round saw together:

1. Fitted **2026-05-27**, n=**103**, training accuracy **0.971**, confusion `{tp:67, fp:3, fn:0, tn:33}` — a memorised fit on a tiny set, now **73 days old**.
2. `t6_promotion` weight is exactly **0.0** — one fifth of the feature vector is dead.
3. `anti_template` is **negative** (−1.577), so scoring higher on anti-template makes a fire *less* likely to be STRIKING — the opposite of its stated purpose, and it is the feature most relevant to grooves.
4. Its own commit (`ab1b8a6`) records that `anti_template` is **unstable across time**: −1.577 deployed vs −0.430 on a same-day refit of nominally identical data, because `score_v2.py` uses rolling windows that depend on "now".
5. STRIKING has **halved** over 24 days (A2) with no intervention explaining the shape.
6. The corpus it scores changed underneath it at R29 — documents got 3× longer — and `length_structure` is its second-largest weight.

**The chain:** scorer selects STRIKING → STRIKING is the exemplar pool → the exemplar pool is what R31/R32 spent two rounds on. A pool of ten near-identical documents all scoring top-10 is a **scorer failure before it is a dedup failure**. R32 deduped what the scorer chose; it did not make the scorer choose better.

This is already ranked #2 in R33's C1. **This round's evidence promotes it to #1** — ahead of the fidelity gate — because it is upstream of the endpoint we just built.
