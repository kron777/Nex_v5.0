### The threshold, and a defect in my own rule

The pre-registered rule was *"smallest even percentage strictly greater than the
observed p99"*. Applied: p99 = 22% → **threshold 24%**.

**But applying that same rule to R31's own data (p99 = 22%) gives 24%, and R31 chose
25%.** My operationalisation does not reconstruct the decision it claimed to formalise.
Reported rather than quietly swapped:

| candidate | source | breaches in 432 clean windows |
|---|---|---|
| 22% | at p99 | 1 / 432 = 0.23% |
| **24%** | **committed rule** | **0 / 432 = 0.00%** |
| 25% | R31-consistent (p99 + 3) | 0 / 432 = 0.00% |

**Both 24% and 25% give zero breaches on a corpus with no convergence in it — which is
the correct behaviour, not insensitivity.** The sensitivity test is whether a *known
real* convergence clears them: the `societal` event peaked at **26–34%** and `societal`
is in no version of the register list, so its DF is unaffected by the refit. **It
clears both.**

**Recommendation: 24%, per the committed rule** — it is 2 points nearer the clean
ceiling than 25% and still admits the only real convergence on record. But the
difference is one belief at N=50, i.e. pure quantisation, so **the refit round may
reasonably keep 25% and lose nothing.** If the alarm's resolution ever matters, the
real fix is a wider window, not a finer threshold.

3. **Replace median and p90 (12→16, 18→20) whatever the threshold decision is.** Rounds

## 2.4 Verdict

**EMERGING PATTERN (2–3 additional qualifying chains).** Per the pre-registered
threshold: name each, recommend a targeted note, **not** a process rule. A rule
mandating re-measurement of every cited figure would be disproportionate — half the
candidate chains were already refreshed without one.

**The recommendation is narrower and follows from what distinguishes the two groups.**
Every re-measured chain describes something *obviously* live — an accrual rate, a row
count, a query mix. Every stale chain describes something that *feels* settled: a
bootstrap floor, a variance decomposition, a regime-change magnitude. **The failure
mode is not "citing numbers"; it is treating a derived statistical property as a
constant.** Those are exactly the figures that drift silently, because nothing about
them looks time-varying.

**Proposed note (not a rule):** when a round leans on a *derived statistical property*
from an earlier round — a floor, a variance share, a stability bar, a fitted quantile —
recompute it or mark it `unverified carry-over`. Rates and counts largely take care of
themselves.
