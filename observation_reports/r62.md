### Why R61 said 34–39/40 and this says 27

R61 measured retention of a **resampled fit against the full-corpus fit**. The
resample is a subset of the corpus the reference was fitted on, so the two share
documents and the comparison is partly self-scoring. **R61 named this in a caveat**
(*"the resamples are drawn from the same 481 docs as the reference fit, which inflates
retention… indicative, not a clean replication"*) **and then reported 34–39/40 as the
headline anyway.** Naming a limitation is not the same as honouring it. This round
pre-registered the stricter test precisely to close that gap, and it closed it in the
opposite direction to the one I expected.

**Correction to R61's central claim: the 1,000-doc floor gates BOTH the list and the
quantiles, not the quantiles alone.**

## C. The register is not stationary — OBSERVED, and this is new

Is the 27/40 general temporal drift, or is it the R57 boundary sitting near the
midpoint? Disentangled:

| split | n per half | retention |
|---|---|---|
| **across** the R57 boundary (pre vs post) | 225 / 257 | **26/40** |
| within pre-R57, **time**-ordered | 112 | **24/40** |
| within post-R57, **time**-ordered | 128 | **24/40** |
| within pre-R57, **random** (mean of 5) | 112 | **28.8/40** |
| within post-R57, **random** (mean of 5) | 128 | **29.2/40** |

**Two conclusions.**

**1. R57 is not the cause.** Retention across the R57 boundary (26/40) is *better* than
within-regime time-ordered splits (24/40). The regime change is not what breaks the
fit.

**2. The register drifts.** At matched n, time-ordered splits score ~5 points below
random splits — consistently, in both regimes. Random splits measure sampling noise;
time-ordered splits measure noise **plus** drift. The gap is the drift.

**REASONED, and flagged as such:** more documents shrink the sampling-noise component
but not the drift component. The gap does narrow with n (≈5 points at n≈120/half,
≈3.6 at n=241/half), so part of it is small-sample. **I cannot determine from this data
whether n=1,000 clears 33/40. It is genuinely borderline** — extrapolating gives
time-ordered somewhere around 31–33. **That is a coin-flip, not a schedule.**
