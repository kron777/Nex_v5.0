## A2 — the quantitative guard: flat and branch-agnostic — OBSERVED

Register vocabulary should be **flat over time** and **spread across branches**; an attractor
spikes and concentrates. Burst ratio = max daily DF / median daily DF; branch entropy is
normalised against the corpus's own (1.790 over 6 branches, n=1041):

| token | burst ratio | branch entropy | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `item` | **1.2** | 0.960 | flat, branch-agnostic → **register** |
| `aligns` | **1.4** | 1.007 | **register** |
| `discusses` | **1.4** | 0.987 | **register** |
| `highlights` | **2.0** | 0.929 | **register** |
| **`societal`** | **22.7** | **0.808** | **ATTRACTOR — must never be excluded** |
| **`advancements`** | **3.8** | **0.803** | **ATTRACTOR — must never be excluded** |

**The two known attractors hold the two lowest branch entropies of the twenty tokens
tested.** The four additions sit at 0.93–1.01, i.e. distributed exactly as the corpus is.

## A3 — other candidates surveyed, and why almost all were rejected

The rest of the frame family, on the same clean window: `suggests` 6.6%, `underscores` 5.4%,
`reflects` 2.1%, `discussing` 1.9%, `mentions` 1.9%, `reinforces` 1.6%, `titled` 1.2%,
`notes` 0.7%, everything else ≤0.5%.

**None added.** Against a 25% threshold a token at 6.6% cannot drive a reading, so excluding
it buys nothing and costs sensitivity. **The cut is exactly at "could plausibly drive a
reading."**

**The high-DF tokens I deliberately did NOT add** — `engagement` 19.1%, `systems` 16.7%,
`dynamics` 12.7%, `understanding` 11.8%, `complexity` 11.3%, `research` 10.6%,
`technologies` 9.9%:

**These are content nouns, and a convergence onto a content noun is precisely what this
metric exists to catch.** Their usage is subject-bearing — *"probability systems"*, *"social
dynamics"*, *"artificial intelligence research"* — unlike `item`, which never carries a
subject. **Several are flat and branch-agnostic and would have passed a purely statistical
test.** They are excluded from the patch on the semantic criterion, which is the one that
matters here: **frame vocabulary is excludable; content vocabulary is not, however habitual
it becomes.**

**This also retroactively explains R50's removals.** `changes` and `systems` were dropped as
contaminant-induced — both content nouns. The principle was already implicit in that
decision; this round states it.
