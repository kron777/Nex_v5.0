## C. What this unblocks — stated, not shipped

### C.1 The F2 sign-flip case is **WEAKER** than R40 presented — OBSERVED

The clean window cannot be used directly: **only 10 of the 120 labelled fires fall inside it**, and the label set has 4 positives total. But the labelled set answers the question a different way — **41 of its 120 fires (34.2%) ARE the contaminant, and all 41 are labelled ordinary.**

Recomputing R40 §B.3's AUCs with the contaminant removed:

| | all 120 | **contaminant excluded (n=79)** |
|---|---|---|
| F1 length_structure | 0.4752 | 0.5767 |
| **F2 anti_template** | **0.8093** (perm p=0.0173) | **0.7183** (perm p=0.0768) |
| F4 self_witnessing | 0.4903 | 0.6167 |
| F5 unprompted | 0.6207 | 0.6267 |
| deployed score | 0.4537 | 0.5417 |

(The "all 120" column reproduces R40 §B.3 exactly for F1/F2/F4/F5.)

**Mean F2: contaminant 0.6994, non-contaminant 0.9253.**

**Answer: it was partly fitting the contaminant.** F2's AUC falls 0.8093 → 0.7183, and **its permutation p goes 0.0173 → 0.0768 — it loses significance at α=0.05.** Roughly half the measured discriminative power came from 41 near-duplicate copies of one document that are low-F2 and unanimously ordinary.

**Two things this does NOT say, and I will not overclaim either.** It does **not** say the sign is right — R48 §B.3 measured the contaminant scoring 4.6× more STRIKING *because* its low F2 met a negative weight, which is direct evidence the sign is wrong, and that evidence does not depend on labels at all. And it does **not** rehabilitate these labels: **R43 confirmed κ=0 and discarded R40 §B.1 and §B.3.** With 4 positives both columns sit inside an enormous permutation null. **The correct reading is that the label-based case for the flip has largely evaporated, while the label-free mechanistic case from R48 stands untouched.** **No weight was changed.**
