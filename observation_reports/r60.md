### Why r36 is the one flip-risk

R36 controlled the **length** channel (truncating both periods to common length) and
found 30–55% of the R29 step was artefact, concluding **"a real +4 to +6 pt step
survives, so the alarm was not false."** In the same round it identified the
**exclusion-list channel** as a separate defect (§207) — and did **not** control for
it. That channel inflates the post-R29 side specifically, because the register turned
over at R29 and the list did not follow.

**So the residual +4 to +6 pt step is exactly the size the uncontrolled channel could
account for.** Correcting it makes R36 *more* right that the step was artefact and
*wrong* on the sub-claim that a real step survived. Either way the number moves.

## Carry-forward

1. **The audit's own finding is that the process worked.** Ten rounds independently
   flagged the instrument; two (R49, R50) ran multi-variant sensitivity checks
   unprompted; R52 attached a caveat to its own tripwire and R53 enforced it as a
   gate. **Exposure is one section of one round because nobody trusted the number.**
   That is the reason this is LOCALISED and not corpus-wide.
2. **The failure that did occur was of magnitude, not direction.** Every round said
   "little headroom, 1–7 points"; it was 14 points of inflation. Qualitative flags
   propagated correctly across ten rounds; **no round ever re-measured the size of the
   defect it was citing.** A flag repeated without re-measurement decays into folklore
   — R50's estimate was still being quoted verbatim three rounds later.
3. **Pre-registering the asymmetry was what made this audit cheap.** Establishing
   before measurement that staleness inflates *only* — and then testing the one channel
   that could void it — collapsed 24 rounds of potential re-reads to one. Without it
   every below-threshold reading would have needed individual assessment.
4. **The pre-registered rule needed extension once, and I logged it rather than
   quietly widening it.** §C records the literal and extended counts side by side. The
   verdict is the same under both; had it differed, the honest report would have been
   the ambiguity, not a choice.
