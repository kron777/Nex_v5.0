# NEX5 — Round 52: the architecture question

# **The framing is wrong in a useful way. NEX does not produce unrelated beliefs — it produces related beliefs and never puts them in the same room.** Measured: when NEX forms a thought, **0.62 of its 9 context beliefs share even one content token with the subject it is thinking about, and 59.2% of fires get zero** — while a median of **90 relevant priors already sit in the 30-day pool, unretrieved**.
# **One root cause, five documented symptoms: there is no associative recall anywhere in the system.** Every retrieval path selects by recency or by a static ordering. Not one selects by *relevance to what is being thought about*.
# **D: give the fountain relevance retrieval. That is the first move, and it makes Design 2 the same project.**

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Assessment/design only. No restart, no fire-path change, nothing shipped.**

---

## A. The gap

### A.1 The framing — CHALLENGED — OBSERVED

The brief's premise is that beliefs are "one-off reactions to whatever feed item was in front of it". **The data says otherwise.**

**Subjects over one week** (`fountain_events.focal_item`, 2,215 fires that bind one):

| | |
|---|---|
| distinct subjects | 1,416 |
| fires per subject | mean 1.56, max 7 |
| subjects fired **more than once** | **549** |
| fires landing on a once-only subject | 867 / 2,215 = **39.1%** |

**61% of fires land on a subject NEX touches more than once.** It revisits *"When Feature Flags Do and Don't Make Sense"* seven times, *"Melatonin impairs morning cognition"* six.

**And same-subject fires produce genuinely related text** (since 08-01, 1,416 same-subject pairs vs 4,000 random):

| | median j | p90 | j>0.3 | j>0.4 | j>0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **same subject** | **0.1100** | 0.2742 | 0.0798 | 0.0403 | 0.0212 |
| random pairs | 0.0714 | 0.1304 | 0.0280 | 0.0112 | 0.0045 |
| enrichment | 1.5× | 2.1× | **3×** | **4×** | **5×** |

**So the corpus is not a pile of singletons. It already clusters.** The clusters are simply never linked.

**One honest qualification, and it matters:** the revisits are **bursts, not accumulation**. Median gap between same-subject fires is **1.1 h**; **97.7% fall within 6 h**. NEX returns to a subject while the feed item is still sitting in the ingestion buffer, then never again. So the clustering it has is a property of the input window, not of memory. **That distinction is the whole round.**

### A.2 Ingestion, fountain, or retrieval? — **RETRIEVAL** — OBSERVED

| candidate | evidence | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Ingestion too diverse** | 327,945 `sense_events` in 7 days (`emerging_tech.hn` 58,650, `crypto.exchanges` 25,138…). Material is *abundant*, not scarce | **not the blocker** |
| **Fountain never revisits** | it does — 549 subjects revisited in a week, up to 7× | **not the blocker** |
| **Retrieval never pulls related priors** | see below | **THIS** |

`_retrieve_context_beliefs` (`generator.py:1907`) — the function that fills the prompt with what NEX "already knows" — orders by:

```sql
ORDER BY (b.created_at + (COALESCE(bb.boost_value,1.0) - 1.0) * ?) DESC LIMIT ?
```

**Recency plus a boost bonus. There is no query term. The focal item is not consulted.** Source caps and a per-branch cap add diversity; a random seed sample adds variety. **Nothing anywhere in the function scores relevance to the subject of the fire.**

**The measured cost, over 120 real recent fires:**

| | context beliefs sharing ≥1 content token with the focal item | fires with **zero** relevant context |
|---|---|---|
| **today — recency retrieval** | **0.62 of 9** | **71/120 = 59.2%** |
| **counterfactual — same pool ranked by overlap, 30-day window** | **8.87 of 9** | **0/120 = 0.0%** |

**And the priors are already there: a median of 90 relevant beliefs sit in the 30-day pool at the moment of each fire (p90 188, max 313), and none of them are retrieved.**

**This is a retrieval property, not an ingestion or generation property.** NEX has the material and does not fetch it.

### A.3 This also corrects R51 — OBSERVED

R51 concluded the semantic graph is dead because *"the corpus has nothing to connect"*. **That generalisation was too strong, and the cause was the same one.** `edge_generator` selects its candidates the same way — `created_at >= now-7d ORDER BY created_at DESC LIMIT 100`. Re-running **the same scorer on the same corpus**, changing only the candidate query:

| candidate selection | median j | max j | refines (j>.5) | supports (j>.4) | opposes (j>.3+) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **recency** (live) | 0.0200 | 0.4211 | **0** | 1 | 4 |
| **relevance** (100 best overlap, 30 d) | **0.0909** | **0.6667** | **8** | **45** | **39** |

**R51 reported `refines`' j>0.5 gate as "above the corpus ceiling — structurally unreachable". Under relevance selection it is met.** The ceiling was a property of the *candidate window*, not of the corpus.

**What survives from R51 unchanged:** the harmonizer's pinned scan; the false-positive character of the recent `opposes` rows; and **contradiction specifically** — the conflict scan found ~1 detectable conflict per 19,900 pairs and it was a false positive. *Relatedness* is recoverable; *contradiction* still looks genuinely absent. **REASONED:** an observation stream plausibly has few propositional contradictions even when well-indexed; I have not disproven that.

### A.4 The pattern is system-wide — OBSERVED

| component | how it selects | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `_retrieve_context_beliefs` | `ORDER BY created_at + boost DESC` | 59.2% of fires get zero relevant context |
| `edge_generator:108` | `created_at >= now-7d ORDER BY created_at DESC LIMIT 100` | semantic gates never met (§A.3) |
| `harmonizer:142` | `ORDER BY tier ASC LIMIT 200` | pinned to 200 tier-3 beliefs from mid-May (R51) |
| `_build_recent_striking_block` | top-10 by score **in last 24 h** | recency-bounded |
| **`BeliefRetriever:70`** — *the one relevance scorer* | `ORDER BY tier ASC, confidence DESC LIMIT 500` | **see below** |

**The single component that does relevance is crippled by the same pathology.** `BeliefRetriever` scores keyword overlap × confidence with a 60/40 activation blend — real associative retrieval — but it pre-filters its candidates with `ORDER BY tier ASC, confidence DESC LIMIT 500`. Measured, that pool is:

```
tier 1: 303   tier 2: 40   tier 3: 157   tier 6: 0
eligible overall: 6,177 — the pool reaches 500 = 8.1%
tier-3 eligible: 5,172   tier-6 eligible (the fountain's own output): 662
```

**Zero tier-6 beliefs. The only relevance-retrieval component in NEX cannot see anything NEX has ever concluded** — it sees keystones, bedrock and seeds. And per R46 its sole caller is `stage_capability/tools.py:169`, the **chat** path.

**REASONED, and this is the round's thesis:** NEX has no associative recall. Every path answers *"what happened recently"* or *"what is foundational"*. Nothing answers *"what is this about, and what do I already know about that?"* R44's cut influence loop, R51's dead semantic graph, R51's frozen T8, R50's starved contradiction pressure, and A.2's empty context are **five symptoms of that one absence.**

## B. The dependency order — your model is right, but step 1 is already done

Jon's order: (1) beliefs about the same things → (2) semantic edges → (3) mutual influence → (4) network surface.

**The ordering is correct. The error is in believing NEX is stuck at (1).** §A.1 measures step 1 as substantially satisfied: 549 revisited subjects a week, same-subject text 3–5× enriched, a median of 90 relevant priors per fire. **What is missing is a step (0) nobody has named: associative recall — the ability to fetch what you already know about the thing in front of you.** Insert it before (2) and the rest follows in the order given.

**Is there a cheaper path — cluster on top of the existing 139,573 token-overlap edges? No.** — REASONED from OBSERVED: those edges were built by `edge_generator` from **recency-selected candidates** (`§A.4`). So `cross_domain`/`synthesises` encode *"these two beliefs were written near each other in time and share two nouns"*. **It is a temporal-adjacency graph wearing a similarity graph's schema.** Clustering it would recover NEX's daily news cycle, not its subjects. The cheap path is not available — but the expensive one is cheaper than it looks, because the missing piece is a query, not a corpus.

## D. My recommendation — one thing

> ## Give the fountain associative recall: retrieve context beliefs by relevance to the focal item, alongside recency — and fix `BeliefRetriever`'s pool so it can see tier 6.

### Why it is first — REASONED from OBSERVED

It is the **single root cause of five separately-documented failures** (§A.4). Every one of them has been chased individually across rounds 44–51 and each time the local fix was declined as low-yield — correctly, because each was a symptom. **This is the cause.** It unblocks, in measured terms:

- **Context relevance:** 0.62 → 8.87 of 9 (§A.2), 59.2% → 0% of fires with no relevant prior.
- **The semantic graph:** the same scorer goes from 0/1/4 to 8/45/39 gate-hits per 20,000 pairs (§A.3) — R51's "dead" verdict was measuring the candidate window.
- **R44's influence loop:** beliefs would finally shape the next thought *because they are about it*, which is what that loop was for.
- **Reading 1 in §C** becomes real in the same motion.

### The damper — MANDATORY, R44/R45 rule — REASONED

**This change creates exactly the loop R46 catalogued, and it must be damped before it ships.** Retrieving relevant priors → writing a new belief on that subject → which becomes a relevant prior → the subject self-reinforces. That is the residue-ghost groove at semantic level, and it is more dangerous here than in R46's design because relevance is *self-confirming* in a way random traversal is not.

Three dampers, all from precedent already in the codebase:

1. **Dedicated slot, never merged into the oversample** — R46's HOT lesson. Cap relevance at **3 of the 9** context slots; the other 6 stay recency. A subject can influence but never dominate.
2. **A per-focal-item "already shown" set**, so the same prior cannot be served to consecutive fires on the same subject — this directly targets the 6-hour burst pattern in §A.1, which is where a groove would form first.
3. **Never rank by a stored score** — R46's residue-ghost lesson. Rank by overlap computed at query time, not by `weight` or `boost`.

### Falsifier — how I would know in 48 h — pre-registrable

**Primary endpoint (the thing being bought):** fraction of fires whose context contains ≥1 belief sharing a content token with the focal item. **Today 0.408 (measured). Predict ≥ 0.90. Falsifier: < 0.70 at 48 h.**

**Two revert tripwires, both on instruments that already exist:**
- **maxDF\* breaches 25%** on the crystallized corpus → the groove formed; revert. *(Note the caveat: R50 found maxDF\* currently reads 18–24% on register vocabulary with only 1–7 points of headroom. **The register refit around 08-21 should land before or with this change, or the tripwire is not trustworthy.** That is a real sequencing dependency, not a nicety.)*
- **EXPLAIN on-subject fidelity < 0.65** — C2's own trigger, still instrumented.

**Secondary watch, no tripwire:** semantic-edge creation per week. If §A.3 is right it should move off zero; if it does not, my A.3 inference was wrong and the graph problem is deeper than candidate selection.

### The honest cost

**Not a one-constant change. Not a month of migration. Days, and one of them is risk analysis.**

- The relevance query itself: a new method in `generator.py`, ~40 lines, same shape as the existing block builders. **No schema change, no migration, no new table** — the data is already there and already indexed by `created_at`; the scoring is token overlap the codebase computes in four places already.
- Fixing `BeliefRetriever`'s pool (`ORDER BY tier ASC, confidence DESC LIMIT 500` → a bounded relevance pre-filter): one clause, but it changes the **chat** path's behaviour, so it needs its own read.
- **The real cost is not code, it is the token budget and the groove risk.** Three relevance slots displace three recency slots in a prompt that R37 measured at 3.3 KiB/fire. That is affordable. The groove is the thing to watch, and it is why the dampers are mandatory rather than advisory.
- **One restart. One change. The usual discipline.**

**If I were forced to shrink it:** fix `BeliefRetriever`'s pool alone. It is one clause, it is on the chat path only so the fountain stays untouched, and it would let Jon *see* whether relevance retrieval over the real corpus returns anything worth having — before betting the fire path on it. **That is the cheap probe, and I would run it first.**

## E. Design 2 — RESHAPE, and it becomes the same project

**Keep the goal, kill the mechanism.**

R46's Design 2 traverses `belief_edges` at `weight ≥ 0.8` to bring in beliefs beyond the recency window. **Its instinct is exactly right and this round vindicates it** — reaching past recency is precisely what is missing. **Its mechanism is unsound**, and now for a sharper reason than R51 gave: those edges are not weak semantic relations, they are **temporal-adjacency records** (§B). Traversing them retrieves beliefs that were written at a similar time, which is what the fountain already has.

R46 also found `weight ≥ 0.8` leaves zero semantic edges and forced a choice between quality and pool size. **That dilemma dissolves — it was an artefact of trying to route relevance through a graph that does not encode it.**

**Reshaped: replace edge-traversal with direct relevance retrieval — which is §D.** Same objective (priors beyond the recency window), no dependency on the graph, no fan-out cap needed, no edge-quality filter needed. The dampers R46 derived (dedicated slot, never rank by stored weight, `ORDER BY RANDOM()` over eligible) **carry across intact** and are reused verbatim in §D.

**This resolves a standing Jon decision by making it moot: the `weight ≥ 0.8` semantic-quality tradeoff no longer needs an answer.** Design 2 and §D are one project, and the graph becomes a *consequence* of relevance retrieval rather than a prerequisite for it.
