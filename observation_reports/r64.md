# NEX5 — Round 64: persistence filtering FAILS, and the exclusion-list approach has no bottom

# **VERDICT AGAINST THE PRE-REGISTERED FAILURE CLAUSE (`78ccfe1`): PERSISTENCE FILTERING DOES NOT CLEAR THE BAR.**
# **Two of the three failure conditions fire, at both k, independently.**
# **(b) UNDER-EXCLUSION: k=3 → 80.7%, k=4 → 91.1%, against a ≥50% failure bar.** The lists are too small; excluding the top-18 simply promotes term #19. `systems` drives 110 of 192 held-out windows at k=3; `engagement` drives 132 at k=4. Same defect as today's `item` at 30%, different token.
# **(c) SENSITIVITY: the ≤1% false-breach threshold lands at 28% (k=3) and 30% (k=4), both above the 26% floor.** A threshold the documented `societal` convergence would not have cleared is not an alarm.
# **(a) reading agreement PASSES at both (2.00 pp) — and that turns out to be worthless.** The two lists agree because they are **equally blind**: both readings are driven by the same unexcluded register token. **My own pre-registered bar is necessary but not sufficient, and running it is how I found that out.**
# **ITEM 3 ANSWERED — the third pre-registered outcome fires: term retention is the wrong proxy and should be retired.** Retention and reading agreement move in **opposite** directions (k=3: 55.6% retention / 2.00 pp; top-40: 67.5% / 4.00 pp). **33/40 was never measuring the thing it was used to gate.**
# **THE FINDING THAT OUTRANKS THE k QUESTION: under-exclusion does not fall with list size.** It plateaus at **25–40% and never approaches zero, even at top-200** — `nature` and `chill` still drive readings with 200 terms excluded. **The register has no bottom, because a list fitted on past documents cannot cover a register that drifts.** That is R63's trend, re-emerging as an irreducible floor.
# **R63's persistence number was over-read — by me.** Its 83.3% measured survival into the other half's **top-40** (a superset). Agreement between two independently fitted **persistence lists** is **55.6%**. The design needed the latter.

## A. Setup — OBSERVED

Clean post-R29 corpus, n=482. **Lists fitted on the first half (n=241); every evaluation
on the held-out half (n=241).** Nothing is both fitted and scored on the same documents.

## B. Item 1 — selecting k

### B.1 Under-exclusion — PRIMARY — the failure

Fraction of held-out rolling-50 windows whose maxDF\* **driver is itself a persistent
token**, i.e. standing register the list failed to exclude:

| list | terms | under-exclusion | top drivers (of 192 windows) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **persistence k=3** | 18 | **80.7%** | `systems` 110 · `both` 24 · `complexity` 15 |
| **persistence k=4** | 13 | **91.1%** | `engagement` 132 · `within` 16 · `complexity` 9 |
| shipped v2 (for scale) | 38 | **100.0%** | `item` 189 · `systems` 2 · `aligns` 1 |
| top-40 refitted fresh | 40 | 27.6% | `standing` 53 · `nature` 38 |

**Failure bar was ≥50%. Both k fire, and k=4 is worse than k=3 — smaller is worse,
monotonically.** The pre-registered tie-break (prefer k=3 if within 5 pp) never
applies; they differ by 10.4 pp and both fail outright.

Note the shipped v2 list at **100%** — every single held-out reading is driven by
standing register. That is the live defect, quantified.

### B.2 Over-exclusion — SECONDARY — clean, and not the problem

| k | spike-flagged (max/min sub-window DF ≥ 3) | contains `societal`? |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3 — `within` 3.0, `understanding` 3.4, `without` 3.0 | **no** |
| 4 | 1 — `without` 3.0 | **no** |

Both under the 3-flag disqualifier at k=4 and at the boundary at k=3; neither contains
the one documented real convergence driver. **Over-exclusion is not what killed this
design** — exactly as the pre-registration predicted structurally. The failure is
entirely under-exclusion.

## D. Item 3 — the 33/40 bar — ANSWERED

**33/40 was never a bar.** R31 *observed* that withholding 7 or 14 days retained 33/40
terms with unchanged quantiles; a later round promoted the observation to a threshold.
This round tested whether the proxy tracks the thing it stands in for.

| procedure | \|A\| | \|B\| | term retention | median \|Δ reading\| | bar ≤2 pp | under-exclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| persistence k=3 | 18 | 15 | **55.6%** | **2.00 pp** | PASS | 79.7% |
| persistence k=4 | 13 | 12 | **53.8%** | **2.00 pp** | PASS | 90.1% |
| top-40 (current) | 40 | 40 | **67.5%** | **4.00 pp** | FAIL | 27.6% |
| shipped v2 (frozen) | 38 | 38 | 100.0% | 0.00 pp | PASS | 100.0% |

**Retention and reading agreement move in opposite directions.** The procedure with the
*worst* term retention (k=4, 53.8%) has the *best* reading agreement. **The third
pre-registered outcome fires: there is no clean correspondence, and term retention
should be retired as a stability proxy.**

### But my own bar is also insufficient — found by running it

**k=3 and k=4 pass the ≤2 pp reading-agreement bar because they are equally blind.**
Both lists leave the same high-DF register token in contention, both readings are driven
by it, so both agree. **Agreement achieved by shared blindness is not stability.**

The frozen v2 row makes it starker: comparing a frozen list *to itself* gives 0.00 pp by
construction — a perfect score for the list we know is reading a false breach today.
**The bar cannot evaluate a frozen list at all.**

**Corrected formulation, for whatever round takes this on:** reading agreement is
necessary but not sufficient. A stability criterion must be **reading agreement
*conditional on* low under-exclusion** — the two must be checked together, because
either alone is gameable by a degenerate list.

## E. The finding that outranks the k question — OBSERVED

Under-exclusion as a plain top-K exclusion set grows, fitted on FIT, evaluated on HELD:

| exclusion set | under-exclusion | top drivers |
|---|---|---|
| top-40 | 27.6% | `standing`, `nature` |
| top-60 | 53.1% | `point`, `nature` |
| top-80 | 24.5% | `emergence`, `nature` |
| top-100 | 35.4% | `nature`, `chill` |
| top-140 | 35.9% | `nature`, `chill` |
| **top-200** | **39.6%** | `nature`, `chill` |

**It does not fall. It plateaus at 25–40% and is no better at 200 terms than at 40.**

The mechanism ties directly to R63: the list is fitted on past documents and applied to
future ones. **A term can be outside the fitting half's top-200 and still be a high-DF
driver in the held-out half — because the register drifts at 1.75 terms/day.** No
exclusion set fitted on the past covers the register of the future.

**So the exclusion-list approach has no bottom.** This is not a k problem, not a
construction-method problem, and not a list-size problem. **maxDF\*'s core assumption —
a stationary standing register that can be enumerated in advance — is false for
post-R29 NEX.**

## F. What I did not do

**The anti-tuning clause held.** k=3 and k=4 were named by R63 before this round; I
tested those two and no others. I did not try k=2, k=5, a different sub-window count,
a different top-N, or a hybrid to find something that passes. The top-K sweep in §E is a
**diagnostic of register depth**, reported as a measurement — it is not a candidate and
I am not recommending top-80 because it scored 24.5%.

**And per the pre-registration, I am not proposing a replacement mechanism here.** The
design question reopens. Any successor must be pre-registered on its own terms,
including whether a frozen exclusion list is the right instrument at all.
