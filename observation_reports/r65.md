## C. Item 2 — the replacement, scoped

### C.1 The candidate that clears — self-referencing excess

    excess(t) = DF(t, current 50) − DF(t, trailing 200)
    score     = max over t of excess(t)          — no exclusion list, at all

| | maxDF\* as shipped | **self-referencing excess** |
|---|---|---|
| peak in contamination window | 34% (`societal`) | **30.5% (`societal`)** — global maximum of the replay |
| peak outside | 28% (`aligns`) | 27.0% (`recent`) |
| threshold | 25% (fixed) | **25.0%** (p99 of out-of-window readings) |
| fires inside window | — | 24 / 626 = 3.8%, **23 driven by `societal`** |
| fires outside | — | 2 / 482 = **0.4%** |
| **precision** | **10.8%** | **95.8%** |

**The register tokens that false-breach today all fall below threshold:**

| token | max excess | fires? |
|---|---|---|
| `item` | 21.5% | no |
| `aligns` | 19.5% | no |
| `engagement` | 19.5% | no |
| `systems` | 19.5% | no |
| `discusses` | 16.5% | no |

**Because a register token is high in the current window *and* high in the trailing
baseline, its excess is near zero. A convergence is high now and low before.** The
register/topic discrimination falls out of the construction instead of being
enumerated — which is why drift cannot break it.

200-belief (~2.5-day) baseline, so it was caught. **This design trades "cannot track
drift" for "cannot see slow convergence."** That trade is favourable — drift is
continuous and proven fatal; slow convergence is hypothetical — but the design round must
choose the baseline length deliberately and state what convergence speed it forfeits.
