## D. The label tool — built, sample drawn and frozen

`scripts/label_genius.py`. Terminal, no web UI.

```
  decision  band [0.3,0.70)  wanted  80  drew  80  from 1789 available across 15 days
  high      band [0.8,1.01)  wanted  20  drew  20  from  201 available across 14 days
  low       band [0.0,0.20)  wanted  20  drew  20  from 4073 available across 15 days
Drew 120 fires. FROZEN.
```

Drawn with a fixed seed into `genius_labels`, stratified round-robin across days so no single day's topic can dominate. **Resumable** — progress is committed per keystroke; `q` saves. **`u` undoes** the previous label. **The score is hidden until you answer**, then shown with agree/DISAGREE, so it cannot anchor the judgement.

**Start with:**

```
python3 scripts/label_genius.py
```

(`--status` for progress; `--draw --force` only if you intend to discard the set.)

### D.5 What I will do with the labels — designed before the data exists

1. **Held-out accuracy of the deployed scorer, by band.** Primary read: accuracy in the decision band [0.3, 0.7). **If it is ≥0.75, the scorer is fine at its boundary and a refit is not indicated** — that is the decision the 120 labels buy.
2. **Anchor sanity.** If the 20 high/20 low anchors disagree with the scorer, the fault is calibration-wide, not boundary-local, and the diagnosis changes.
3. **Per-feature discrimination** — AUC of each of F1/F2/F4/F5 against the labels, judged against the frozen baseline in C.5. This is where `anti_template`'s negative weight gets tested: if F2's AUC is ~0.5, the −1.577 is fitting noise.
4. **No refit on 120 labels.** Fitting five weights needs several hundred; the existing 103-example fit with `fn:0` is the cautionary example. 120 settles *whether*, not *what*.
