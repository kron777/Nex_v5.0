## B. The cheap probe — `BeliefRetriever`'s candidate pool

### B.1 The change — one clause, and why the new pool is *right* rather than merely bigger

```diff
- "ORDER BY tier ASC, confidence DESC "
- "LIMIT 500",
+ "ORDER BY confidence DESC "
+ "LIMIT 20000",
```

**The argument, not just the diff.** This statement is **not a ranking** — it runs *before* any scoring. Its only job is to bound how many beliefs reach the scorer. `ORDER BY tier ASC` therefore did not prioritise foundational beliefs; it **decided which beliefs were permitted to be relevant at all**, and it anti-correlates with relevance. Measured, it returned 303 tier-1 + 40 tier-2 + 157 tier-3 and **zero tier-6** — 5,174 tier-3 beliefs fill the cap before tier 6 is ever reached.

**The cap was bounding a cost that does not exist.** The entire eligible set — `tier <= 6`, unpaused, locked or confidence ≥ 0.15 — is **6,184 rows**. Measured: SQL fetch 26.4 ms, in-process scoring 48.8 ms, **~75 ms total against ~3 ms before**. That is noise beside a chat round-trip. At `LIMIT 20000` the cap **no longer binds at all** — every eligible belief reaches the scorer, which is what a relevance retriever is for — and it retains years of headroom at ~65 new tier-6 beliefs/day.

**Two deliberate non-changes:**
- **`confidence DESC` is kept**, so if the cap ever does bind the ordering still degrades sensibly. It is a tie-break now, not a filter.
- **`tier <= 6` is kept.** Admitting tier 7 would add 48,552 rows and change the cost picture entirely. That is a semantic decision about what "her beliefs" means, not a one-clause fix, and it is out of scope.
